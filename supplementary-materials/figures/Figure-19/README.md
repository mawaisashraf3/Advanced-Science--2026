## Panel guide

### (a) Hydrogel harvesting and a powered signal [[196]](../../references/bibliography.md#ref-196)

**(a1)** the hydrogel moisture-electric generator mounted on a breathing mask and skin.

**(a2)** Capacitor charging by arrays with different numbers of generator units.

**(a3)** Electrocardiograms acquired using generator power at the initial test and after four days.

### (b) Textile packaging and on-body sensing [[197]](../../references/bibliography.md#ref-197)

**(b1)** The multiplexed sensing headband with the textile circuit and integrated electronics.

**(b2)** Headband use during cycling, with the battery visible in the inset.

**(b3)** Wearable sweat measurements alongside discrete reference assay results.

### (c) Calibrated hand tracking [[198]](../../references/bibliography.md#ref-198)

**(c1)** The placement of liquid-metal strain traces relative to the finger joints.

**(c2)** Time-resolved fingertip positions estimated by the glove and measured by motion capture for a large-handed participant.

**(c3)** Virtual keypad entry and arithmetic using the tracked hand.

### (d) Wearable pneumatic haptic feedback [[199]](../../references/bibliography.md#ref-199)

**(d1)** The air supply, valves and pressure feedback for one actuator pair.

**(d2)** Virtual ball sorting, showing the wearer and the in-game view.

**(d3)** Participant realism ratings for visual-only, vibration and multimodal feedback.

### (e) Wireless gesture-to-robot control [[200]](../../references/bibliography.md#ref-200)

**(e1)** The wearable glove with strain sensors and a permeable electronics module.

**(e2)** The classification confusion matrix for the 26 letters of the manual alphabet.

**(e3)** Two examples of the robotic hand responding to gestures from the wireless glove, right two frames.

### (f) Task-based validation of a hip exosuit [[201]](../../references/bibliography.md#ref-201)

**(f1)** The hip exosuit and its actuator, battery, tendons and movement sensors during fitting and wear.

**(f2)** Paired assisted and unassisted sit-to-stand repetition counts.

**(f3)** Mean walking cost of transport and its evolution during the walking test.

### (g) Clinical feasibility of assisted grasp [[202]](../../references/bibliography.md#ref-202)

**(g1)** A participant with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis using the exoskeleton to grasp a fork.

**(g2)** Examples of assisted grasp, grip and pinch during the Action Research Arm Test, upper row.

**(g3)** Participant-level changes in grasp, grip and pinch scores with exoskeleton assistance after stroke.

## Scientific explanation

**Functional validation.** The sensing, feedback, and assistive demonstrations define application-level endpoints such as trace agreement, task completion, or movement under load. They give design targets for candidate power systems. Storage chemistry and the source of each device’s energy must be established individually.
